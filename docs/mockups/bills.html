Rules for this screen

  1. The rail gains a Bills item. Scheduled bills and income are not ledger entries and never appear in the register, a balance or a report until they are marked paid.
  2. The horizon lives in the URL as a date. The segmented control writes it; the view is shareable and back-button correct.
  3. Projected balance is the same figure as net worth, carried forward: today’s balance plus everything scheduled between today and the horizon. It comes from the API; the client never adds an occurrence to a balance.
  4. Solid is what happened, dashed is what is scheduled, and the boundary sits exactly at today. Chart geometry may use JS numbers for scaling; every figure a user reads is the API’s string.
  5. A bill due before today and still unpaid is outside the projection, because the projection starts from today’s balance and that money has not moved. Saying so is the point: a forecast that quietly drops an unpaid bill is worse than one that flags it.
  6. The group total is the API’s net figure, not a sum of the rows.
  7. Mark paid creates a real transaction and links it to the occurrence, which then leaves the projection. Nothing else removes an occurrence.

Rules for these dialogs

  1. Two tabs, because the story says bills and income. A bill picks an expense category, income picks an income one; both pick a real account. The sign follows the tab, as in the add-transaction dialog.
  2. Four repeat rules and no more: one-off, weekly, every two weeks, monthly on a day. Anything else is rejected by the contract. Monthly on the 31st lands on the last day of a shorter month.
  3. The dialog says plainly that nothing enters the ledger yet. This is the distinction the whole phase rests on, stated where the user is deciding.
  4. The date and amount are prefilled from the schedule and both editable, because a bill rarely arrives at exactly its scheduled amount.
  5. Confirming creates an ordinary entry through the usual endpoint and links it to the occurrence. The occurrence then drops out of the projection, so the projected total does not change when a bill is paid on time for its scheduled amount.

Rules for these states

  1. The empty state explains what scheduling buys the user, and offers the same action as the header.
  2. The chart is the only cuttable part of this screen. Dropping it leaves the projected figure and the list, both from the same response, and the README records the omission.
  3. Loading keeps the chart’s and list’s geometry. Shimmer stops under reduced motion.
